Accuracy: fixing your weak keys

Accuracy is the part of typing that pays. A typist at a moderate speed with almost no mistakes finishes sooner than a fast typist who must go back and correct every line. In tests that deduct for mistakes, accuracy decides the result.

Find your weak keys

After a typing test on this site, look at the list of words you typed wrongly. Read the wrong letters, not only the words. Most people find that the same two or three keys cause most of their mistakes. Typical ones are:

  • B, V and N, the long reaches of the index fingers.
  • X, Z, Q and P, typed by the weak ring and little fingers.
  • I and O, or E and R, neighbours that get swapped.
  • M and N, which sit together under one finger.

Once you know your weak keys, practise them directly. Ten slow correct taps teach the finger more than a hundred fast doubtful ones.

Slow down to speed up

Take the text you got wrong and type it at half your normal speed, so slowly that a mistake is nearly impossible. Do it three times. Then go a little faster. This feels like going backwards. It is the quickest known way forwards, because the finger learns the correct movement and not the mistake.

Look-alike words

The fifth drill line has pairs such as form and from, trial and trail. They are all real words, so a spelling checker will not catch one typed for the other. Only careful typing does. Read such words fully before your fingers start.

Should you use Backspace?

In daily work, yes: correct a mistake as soon as you see it. In practice, it depends on what you are training. For accuracy practice, correct every mistake, and correct it with the right little finger on Backspace, without looking. For rhythm practice, leave the mistake and go on. In a test, follow the rules of that test. In the test on this site you can correct a word until you press Space. After that the word is counted as typed.

A target to keep

Aim for 97 per cent accuracy or better before you try to go faster. That is about three wrong characters in a hundred. When your accuracy holds there for a week, raise your speed a little and hold it again. Speed and accuracy climb together, one step at a time.
