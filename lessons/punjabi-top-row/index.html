Punjabi InScript: the top row

The top row adds the kanna ਾ, the most used vowel sign in Punjabi, with the bihari ੀ and six common consonants. With the home row and this row you can type many everyday words.

The fingers reach up as they do for the English top row. Vowel signs stay on the left hand and consonants on the right.

Short and long signs sit together

The layout pairs each short sign with its long one under the same finger. The left index finger has the sihari ਿ at home and the bihari ੀ above it, and the aunkar ੁ at home with the dulainkar ੂ above. The little finger has the hora ੋ at home and the kanaura ੌ above, and the ring finger has the lavan ੇ with the dulavan ੈ above.

The pair bindi

The key ] types the dot written at the foot of a letter. It is typed after the consonant: ਜ then the dot gives ਜ਼, as in ਜ਼ਰੂਰ and ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ. The same dot makes ਖ਼, ਗ਼ and ਫ਼. For ਸ਼ and ਲ਼ there are also ready-made keys with Shift, taught in a later lesson.

A few drill words need a Shift letter from a later lesson: ਠ in ਬੈਠ, the full vowel ਈ in ਪਈ and ਗਈ, and ਲ in ਕੋਲ from the bottom row. If you have not learnt them, leave those words out for now, or try them: ਠ is Shift and ', ਈ is Shift and R, ਲ is N.

The kanna is everywhere

The key E will be your busiest key in Punjabi, because it types the kanna. The left middle finger must reach up for it and return at once to the halant key. The pair ਕ then ਾ, right hand then left, should become one smooth roll: ਕਾ, ਰਾ, ਤਾ, ਚਾ.

Common trouble on this row

  • Bihari for sihari. Both belong to the left index finger: sihari at home, bihari above. Practise ਕਿ ਕੀ ਕਿ ਕੀ.
  • ਬ and ਹ swapped. Both are typed by the right index finger. ਹ is straight up from ਰ. ਬ is up and to the left.
  • ਦ and ਡ mixed up. They sound close but are different letters on different keys: ਦ on O, ਡ on [.
